Motivation Assay review Challenge panel Relevance

The Greenleaf Lab turns sequence variation into quantitative measurements of molecular interactions. Massively parallel filter binding makes this especially tangible: thousands of DNA targets can be followed together as Cas9 binds them. Before interpreting an unusual target as interesting biology, how sensitive is its apparent binding to read support and the choice of normalization control?

I built a count-level review workflow for the lab’s public Cas9 experiment. It links each target’s time course to a transparent, adjustable review queue, then uses the authors’ sequence-energy model to assemble a small challenge panel for a future paired assay check. This is an exploratory QC proposal—not evidence of an equipment problem in the published experiment.

How it works

The source is Boyle and colleagues’ 2021 filter-binding study. This view uses the 5 nM dCas9 association count tables: select a guide library, then its perfect target or a single-base mismatch. Binding is inferred from the loss of unbound target reads relative to the supplied control classes. Alternative normalizations stay visible, including values outside 0–1; the page does not fit kinetic rates or reproduce the paper’s full model-fitting pipeline.

Review criteria are deliberately descriptive. Low count support, control sensitivity or continued late change means “inspect or extend this measurement,” not “discard it.” The proposed paired validation below adds the timing, recovery and equipment records that these public data do not contain.

Proposed paired check—not a diagnosed fault

Run the selected panel with the perfect target and both published control classes before and after one recorded protocol or equipment change. Randomize well positions and repeat the pair on an independent day. Why this matters for the lab

The advertised LSRP role pairs filter-binding experiments with maintenance and expansion of their equipment. The useful handoff here is a review list tied to actual reads and a paired-check plan, not a claim that an unusual curve identifies a hardware fault. The workflow could accept a new target-by-time count table with guide, control, concentration and sample annotations; fresh controls and prospective validation would be needed before treating it as an assay acceptance procedure.

Methods note

The source is Figshare 12526157, version 2, accompanying Boyle et al., Science Advances (2021). This is a processed-count reanalysis, not FASTQ reprocessing. The interactive subset contains perfect and single-mismatch targets from the 5 nM association experiment; it does not analyze cleavage, every mismatch class or every concentration. The source’s 91 libraries include a repeated guide, rather than 91 independent guide identities.

Control differences, baseline sensitivity and late-time change are descriptive diagnostics, not confidence intervals or proof of a failed assay. Continued late change may be genuine kinetics. There are no matched maintenance records, and sequencing counts are not biological replicates. Casland parameters derive from this experimental programme; comparison against these data is not a held-out performance test. No clinical guide recommendation is made.

Reproduce the transformations, inspect formulas, data checks and software attribution in the public source repository. Author-model version: 9193bf6. Review thresholds are exploratory choices, requiring assay-specific validation.
